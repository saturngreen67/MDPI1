# Potential Risk Index (PRI) Assessment Report

This assessment integrates the **Hazard Index (HI)**, **Exposure Index (EI)**, and **Vulnerability Index (VI)** to derive the **Potential Risk Index (PRI)** for the listed railway-related impacts. In line with the provided methodology, **EI is consistently 4** across the dataset, indicating high exposure, and the PRI is interpreted on a **0–4 scale**.

## Highest Risks

### PRI 2 — Landslides and debris flow
The highest risk level in the table is **PRI 2**, driven by **Landslides** under multiple impact pathways.

#### 1) Reactive CAPEX due to damages associated to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

This is the most severe combination in the dataset because the **HI is moderate (2)**, exposure is high (**EI 4**), and vulnerability is very high (**VI 5**). The resulting PRI reflects a strong risk signal for damage-related capital expenditure.

#### 2) Increased maintenance due to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

This impact shares the same risk profile as the CAPEX case above. The combination of **moderate hazard**, **high exposure**, and **very high vulnerability** produces a **PRI 2**, indicating elevated maintenance burden associated with landslide conditions.

#### 3) Increased maintenance for damage to the track bed (ballast) due to changes in precipitation
- **Hazard:** Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice)
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 4
- **PRI:** 2

Here, the **HI remains moderate (2)** and exposure is high (**EI 4**), while vulnerability is also high (**VI 4**). This yields a **PRI 2**, showing that precipitation-related changes can materially affect track-bed maintenance requirements.

#### 4) Stop of operations due to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

This operational impact has the same underlying risk structure as the landslide maintenance and CAPEX entries. The **moderate HI**, **high EI**, and **very high VI** combine to produce the highest PRI observed in the table.

## Moderate to Low Risks

### PRI 1 — Temperature variability, heavy precipitation, and wind-related operational disruption
Several impacts fall into the **PRI 1** category. These are characterized by either a lower HI or a lower VI relative to the highest-risk entries, despite consistently high exposure.

#### 1) Increased maintenance due to temperature increase
- **Hazard:** Temperature variability
- **Climate indicator:** Mean annual temperature
- **HI:** 1
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 3
- **PRI:** 1

The risk is limited by the **low HI (1)** and moderate vulnerability (**VI 3**), even though exposure remains high.

#### 2) Stop of operations due to heavy rain
- **Hazard:** Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice)
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 3
- **PRI:** 1

Although the hazard is moderate (**HI 2**) and exposure is high (**EI 4**), the lower vulnerability (**VI 3**) reduces the PRI to **1**.

#### 3) Stop of operations due to strong winds
- **Hazard:** Changing wind patterns
- **Climate indicator:** Annual mean windspeed at 10 m
- **HI:** 1
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 2
- **PRI:** 1

This is a lower-risk operational impact because both the **HI (1)** and **VI (2)** are limited, despite high exposure.

## No Risk

### PRI 0 — Changing precipitation patterns

- **HI:** 0
- **EI:** 4

This entry is assigned **PRI 0** because the **HI is 0**, indicating no relevant hazard variation exists for this driver in the provided table. In accordance with the protocol, this should be treated as **not applicable**, without further inference.

## Conclusion

The PRI assessment shows that the **dominant risks** are associated with **Landslides and debris flow**, particularly where heavy-rain conditions are used as the climate indicator. These impacts consistently combine **moderate HI (2)**, **high EI (4)**, and **very high VI (5)**, resulting in **PRI 2**.

A second tier of risks appears at **PRI 1**, mainly for:
- **Temperature variability**
- **Heavy precipitation-related operational stoppages**
- **Changing wind patterns**

The only **PRI 0** case is the maintenance impact linked to **changing precipitation patterns**, where **HI = 0** indicates no relevant hazard variation for that driver.

## Climate Hazard Assessment for Railway Infrastructure

The table indicates that the railway infrastructure is exposed to a mix of acute and chronic climate hazards, with the most severe risks concentrated in **heat wave** and **droughts**, both rated **Extreme (Hazard Index: 5)**. These represent the highest-priority threats because they combine strong physical impacts with high potential for operational disruption and structural stress.

### Highest-priority hazards

**Heat wave** is the most critical temperature-related acute hazard, with an **Extreme** hazard level and **Hazard Index: 5**. The stated impact model—track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat—suggests direct threats to rail integrity and system reliability.

**Droughts** are equally severe, also classified as **Extreme (Hazard Index: 5)**. The impact model highlights soil desiccation affecting embankment stability and vegetation, indicating major risks to the supporting rail corridor and surrounding ground conditions.

### Significant chronic and resource-related hazards

**Water stress** is assessed as **Very High (Hazard Index: 4)**. Reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations implies operational constraints that may affect upkeep, asset performance, and resilience under prolonged dry conditions.

Two temperature-related chronic hazards are rated at a moderate level but remain important due to their cumulative effects:

- **Changing temperature (air)** is classified as **Medium (Hazard Index: 2)**, with thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components over time.
- **Temperature variability** is also **Medium (Hazard Index: 2)**, reflecting cumulative thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure.

Together, these indicate that gradual and fluctuating thermal conditions may not be as immediately damaging as heat waves, but they can still contribute to long-term degradation.

### Precipitation-related and slope hazards

**Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice)** is rated **Medium (Hazard Index: 2)**. The impact model points to flash flooding and drainage overload affecting track operations, showing that intense precipitation events can still create operational and drainage challenges.

**Landslide** is likewise **Medium (Hazard Index: 2)**, with slope instability triggered by intense precipitation events. This suggests localized but potentially serious risk where rail assets are located near unstable terrain.

### Lower-level wind-related hazard

**Changing wind patterns** are assessed as **Low (Hazard Index: 1)**. The impact model notes minor crosswind effects on train stability, indicating a relatively limited hazard contribution compared with the dominant temperature- and water-related risks.

Overall, the railway’s hazard profile is dominated by **extreme heat and drought-related risks**, with **water stress** also emerging as a major concern.

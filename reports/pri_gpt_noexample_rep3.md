# PRI Assessment Report

## Scope and method

This report assesses the provided PRI data table using the stated framework:

- **PRI = HI × EI × VI**, normalised to a **0–4 scale**
- **EI = 3** is the conservative default where no economic asset valuation is available; however, the supplied table already provides EI values, so those values are used as given
- A dash in **HI** would indicate no relevant hazard variation and would be treated as not applicable; **no dash values are present** in the table

The risks below are **categorised highest first by PRI**.

## 4) Driver interpretation

### Landslides and debris flow
These are the most significant drivers in the table. They consistently combine:
- **HI 2**: moderate hazard intensity
- **EI 4**: high economic exposure
- **VI 5**: very high vulnerability

This combination yields the highest PRI values and affects both **CAPEX** and **OPEX**, as well as **revenues** through operational stoppage.

### Changing precipitation patterns
These risks are material but slightly less severe than landslide-related cases where vulnerability is lower or hazard intensity is not as strong. The table shows both:
- a **zero-HI case** for increased maintenance due to increase in precipitation, which results in **PRI 0**
- a **higher-HI case** for ballast damage due to precipitation changes, which results in **PRI 2**

### Temperature variability
Temperature increase is present as a lower-order maintenance driver:
- **HI 1**
- **EI 4**
- **VI 3**
- **PRI 1**

### Wind patterns
Strong winds produce an operational stoppage risk with:
- **HI 1**
- **EI 4**
- **VI 2**
- **PRI 1**

---

## 5) Conclusion

The table indicates that the **highest PRI risks are concentrated in landslide and debris-flow related drivers**, particularly where heavy rain acts as the climate indicator. These risks affect:
- **Reactive CAPEX**
- **Maintenance OPEX**
- **Operational revenues**

By contrast, **temperature** and **wind** drivers are lower in PRI, and one precipitation-related maintenance item is scored at **PRI 0** because **HI = 0**.

No engineering or mitigation strategies are proposed here, in line with the instruction to avoid speculation.

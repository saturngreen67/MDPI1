### Climate Hazard Assessment for Railway Infrastructure

The climate hazard table highlights the potential risks to railway infrastructure under various climate drivers. This analysis will group hazards by their likelihood and severity, providing an overview of the most critical threats.

#### Acute Temperature-Related Hazards

Heat waves emerge as a significant acute hazard with an **Extreme** Hazard Level (Index: 5). The potential for track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat poses substantial risks to operational integrity. This is further exacerbated by droughts, which also register **Extreme** scores (Index: 5) and contribute significantly to soil desiccation affecting embankment stability.

#### Chronic Temperature-Related Hazards

Temperature variability presents a moderate hazard with a **Medium** Hazard Level (Index: 2). The cumulative effect of thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure highlights the need for proactive maintenance strategies. Additionally, changing temperature patterns register a **Medium** score (Index: 2), indicating potential risks to structural integrity over time.

#### Precipitation-Related Hazards

While droughts and heat waves dominate the hazard landscape, precipitation-related hazards also pose risks. Heavy precipitation is assessed as having a **Medium** Hazard Level (Index: 2), with flash flooding and drainage overload affecting track operations being primary concerns. Changing precipitation patterns are categorized under minimal direct operational impact with a **No Variation** score (Index: 0).

#### Water Stress

Water stress emerges as a significant chronic hazard, registering a **Very High** Hazard Level (Index: 4). Reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations underscores the importance of proactive planning to mitigate these effects.

#### Other Hazards

Changing wind patterns are assessed with a **Low** Hazard Level (Index: 1), primarily due to minor crosswind effects on train stability. Landslides, while registering a **Medium** score (Index: 2), pose localized impact risks that should not be underestimated, especially in mountainous terrain.

#### Summary and Recommendations

In conclusion, droughts, heat waves, and water stress are the most critical climate hazards for railway infrastructure, reflecting high likelihood and severity. It is crucial to develop proactive strategies addressing these acute and chronic threats. Furthermore, comprehensive risk management must consider all hazards, even those with lower scores, as their collective contribution to overall vulnerability can be substantial.

This report underscores the importance of integrating climate hazard assessments into infrastructure planning and maintenance strategies, ensuring resilience against a range of climate-related risks.

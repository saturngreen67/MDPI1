**Potential Risk Index (PRI) Assessment Report**

The analysis integrates the Hazard Index (HI), Exposure Index (EI), and Vulnerability Index (VI) to compute the Potential Risk Index (PRI) for the analyzed infrastructure assets.

**High Risks (PRI 2)**
1. **Landslides**: This risk is driven by a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and very high Vulnerability (VI: 5).
	* Consequences: Reactive CAPEX due to damages associated to landslides and debris flow, increased maintenance for damage to the track bed.
2. **Heavy Precipitation**: This risk is characterized by a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3-5).
	* Consequences: Reactive CAPEX due to damages associated to heavy rains, stop of operations due to heavy rain.

**Moderate Risks (PRI 1)**
1. **Temperature Variability**: This risk presents a low Hazard Index (HI: 1), but high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3).
	* Consequences: Increased maintenance due to temperature increase.
2. **Changing Precipitation Patterns**: This risk is characterized by a low Hazard Index (HI: 0), but high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3).
	* Consequences: Increased maintenance due to changes in precipitation.

**Low Risks**
1. **Storms (winds)**: This risk presents a low Hazard Index (HI: 1), but high Exposure (EI: 4) and low Vulnerability (VI: 2).
	* Consequences: Stop of operations due to strong winds.
2. **Changing Wind Patterns**: This risk is characterized by a low Hazard Index (HI: 1), but high Exposure (EI: 4) and low Vulnerability (VI: 2).
	* Consequences: Stop of operations due to changing wind patterns.

**Conclusion**
The analysis highlights the importance of addressing landslides, heavy precipitation, temperature variability, and changing precipitation patterns in railway infrastructure risk management. These drivers pose significant risks due to their moderate to high Hazard Index values combined with high Exposure and Vulnerability levels.

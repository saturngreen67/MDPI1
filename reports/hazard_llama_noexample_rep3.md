**Climate Hazards Affecting Railway Infrastructure**

The climate hazard table indicates that railway infrastructure is exposed to various climatic drivers, resulting in a range of potential impacts. This report groups the hazards by category and provides an overview of the predicted outcomes.

### Temperature-Related Hazards

The changing temperature (air) driver has been identified as a significant threat, with a Hazard Index score of 2 and a Medium Hazard Level. The impact model suggests that thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components will occur over time. Additionally, temperature variability is also considered a moderate risk, with a Hazard Index of 2 and a Medium Hazard Level, resulting in cumulative thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure.

Heat waves have been identified as an extreme hazard (Hazard Index: 5; Extreme Hazard Level), posing a significant threat to track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat. Conversely, cold waves/frost are considered Not applicable, with no relevant variation in the Hazard Index.

### Precipitation-Related Hazards

Changing precipitation patterns have been found to result in minimal direct operational impact, with a No variation Hazard Level and a Hazard Index of 0. However, droughts are identified as an extreme hazard (Hazard Index: 5; Extreme Hazard Level), affecting soil desiccation and embankment stability.

Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice) has been classified as a medium hazard (Hazard Index: 2; Medium Hazard Level), posing flash flooding and drainage overload risks to track operations. Water stress is also considered a significant threat (Hazard Index: 4; Very High Hazard Level), affecting maintenance and cooling operations.

### Other Hazards

Changing wind patterns have been found to result in minor crosswind effects on train stability, with a Low Hazard Level and a Hazard Index of 1. Storms and landslides are considered Not applicable, with no relevant variation in the Hazard Index.

**Key Findings**

* Temperature-related hazards pose significant risks to railway infrastructure.
* Precipitation-related hazards also present notable threats, particularly droughts and heavy precipitation events.
* Water stress is a critical concern due to reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations.
* The overall risk profile suggests that railway operators must be prepared to address multiple climate-driven hazards simultaneously.

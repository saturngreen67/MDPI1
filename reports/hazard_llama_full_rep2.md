### Climate Hazard Analysis for Railway Infrastructure

The climate hazard analysis for the railway infrastructure highlights significant risks associated with various climate drivers. This report categorizes hazards into three groups based on their impact potential, likelihood, and severity.

#### Temperature-Related Hazards

Temperature variability is a moderate concern, registering a Hazard Index of 2 (Medium) due to cumulative thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure. Changing temperature patterns are also an issue, with a similar medium score (Hazard Index: 2), as they cause thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components over time.

Cold waves/frost pose minimal direct operational impact since they are classified as Not applicable (no relevant hazard variation). Conversely, heat waves are a significant threat, reaching EXTREME levels (Hazard Index: 5) with track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat. 

#### Precipitation-Related Hazards

Changing precipitation patterns do not pose a direct operational impact, as they are categorized under No variation (Hazard Index: 0). However, droughts register EXTREME levels of hazard potential (Hazard Index: 5) with soil desiccation affecting embankment stability and vegetation.

Heavy precipitation events also have a significant but moderate impact (Hazard Index: 2), mainly through flash flooding and drainage overload affecting track operations. This indicates that while the overall change in total precipitation is minimal, shifts in intensity could pose operational risks if drainage systems are overwhelmed.

#### Other Hazards

Wildfires do not directly affect rail infrastructure, as they are classified under Not applicable (no relevant hazard variation). Storms also do not significantly impact railway operations due to minor effects on train stability. Landslides and slope instability triggered by intense precipitation events present moderate hazards (Hazard Index: 2).

#### Water Stress

Water stress has a significant impact on the operational integrity of the infrastructure, with a VERY HIGH hazard level (Hazard Index: 4). Reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations poses substantial risks to ecological balance, soil desiccation, and vegetation loss.

### Conclusion

Droughts and heat waves constitute the most critical climate hazards for railway infrastructure, registering EXTREME levels of hazard potential. Water stress also presents a significant threat with VERY HIGH hazard levels. However, it is essential to consider all climate drivers, including those with moderate or low scores, as they can significantly contribute to overall risk if their exposure and vulnerability are not properly managed.

The comprehensive analysis highlights the need for proactive measures to mitigate these hazards, ensuring the long-term resilience of railway infrastructure under changing climatic conditions.

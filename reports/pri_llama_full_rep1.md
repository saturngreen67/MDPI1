**Potential Risk Index (PRI) Assessment Report**

The analysis integrates the Hazard Index (HI), Exposure Index (EI), and Vulnerability Index (VI) to compute the Potential Risk Index (PRI) for the analyzed infrastructure assets.

**High Risks (PRI 2)**

1. **Landslides**: This risk is characterized by a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and extremely high Vulnerability (VI: 5). The consequences include reactive CAPEX due to damages associated with landslides and debris flow, as well as increased maintenance for damage to the track bed.
2. **Heavy Precipitation**: This risk is driven by a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderately high Vulnerability (VI: 3-5). The impacts include reactive CAPEX due to structural damages, increased maintenance for damage to the track bed, and potential operational stoppages.
3. **Changing Precipitation Patterns**: This risk is characterized by a low Hazard Index (HI: 0) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderately high Vulnerability (VI: 3). The consequences include increased maintenance due to increase in precipitation.

**Moderate Risks (PRI 1)**

1. **Temperature Variability**: This risk is driven by a low Hazard Index (HI: 1) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderately high Vulnerability (VI: 3). The consequences include increased maintenance due to temperature increase.
2. **Storms (Winds)**: This risk is characterized by a low Hazard Index (HI: 1) combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderate Vulnerability (VI: 2). The impacts include stop of operations due to strong winds.

**No Risk (PRI 0)**

Impacts related to general **Changing precipitation patterns** registered a PRI of 0, as the Hazard Index was low (HI: 0).

**Conclusion**

The analysis highlights that landslides and heavy precipitation are the primary drivers of high risks for the analyzed infrastructure assets. The Exposure Index is consistently high across all climate indicators, indicating that the infrastructure is exposed to various climate-related hazards. The Vulnerability Index varies across different climate indicators, with extremely high vulnerability observed for landslides.

The results suggest that proactive measures should be taken to mitigate the impacts of these risks. This includes investing in hazard-resistant design and construction practices, as well as developing early warning systems for landslides and heavy precipitation events.

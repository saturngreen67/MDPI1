# Climate Hazards to Railway Infrastructure

The provided table categorizes various climate-related hazards affecting railway infrastructure, grouped based on the type of impact they pose.

## Temperature-Related Hazards

Temperature variability (Hazard Index: 2) and changing temperature (air) (Hazard Index: 2) are both classified as Medium risks. These drivers contribute to thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components over time. Heat waves (Hazard Index: 5, Hazard Level: Extreme) pose the highest risk among these, with potential for track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat.

## Precipitation-Related Hazards

Changing precipitation patterns have a minimal direct operational impact (Hazard Index: 0), but heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice) poses a Medium risk (Hazard Index: 2). This includes flash flooding and drainage overload affecting track operations. Droughts (Hazard Index: 5, Hazard Level: Extreme) also pose an extreme risk due to soil desiccation affecting embankment stability and vegetation.

## Water-Related Hazards

Water stress is a Very High risk (Hazard Index: 4), with potential for reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations. Droughts contribute to this, as mentioned earlier.

## Other Hazards

Changing wind patterns pose a Low risk (Hazard Index: 1) due to minor crosswind effects on train stability. Landslide is also classified under Medium risk (Hazard Index: 2), triggered by intense precipitation events.

## Not Applicable Hazards

Cold waves/frost, wildfires, and storm (winds) do not have a significant impact based on the provided data, with Hazard Indices of - or -- respectively. These are classified as Not applicable.

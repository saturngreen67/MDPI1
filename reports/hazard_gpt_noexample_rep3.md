# Railway Climate Hazard Assessment

## Overview
The railway infrastructure shows a mixed hazard profile across climate drivers, with several **high-concern hazards** concentrated in heat, drought, and water-related stressors. Other drivers present only **low to medium** impacts, while some are marked **Not applicable** or **No variation**, indicating no relevant hazard variation for this infrastructure under the provided table.

## High-priority hazards

### Heat-related stress
The most severe heat-related hazard is **Heat wave**, with a **Hazard Level: Extreme** and **Hazard Index: 5**. The impact model indicates **track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat**, making this a critical operational and structural risk.

### Dryness and water availability
Two major hazards are associated with water scarcity:
- **Water stress** has a **Hazard Level: Very High** and **Hazard Index: 4**, driven by **reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations**.
- **Droughts** are rated **Hazard Level: Extreme** with **Hazard Index: 5**, linked to **soil desiccation affecting embankment stability and vegetation**.

These indicate that prolonged dry conditions may significantly affect both infrastructure stability and maintenance capacity.

## Moderate hazards

### Thermal change and variability
Longer-term thermal effects are present but less severe than extreme heat:
- **Changing temperature (air)** has **Hazard Level: Medium** and **Hazard Index: 2**, associated with **thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components over time**.
- **Temperature variability** also has **Hazard Level: Medium** and **Hazard Index: 2**, reflecting **cumulative thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure**.

### Heavy precipitation and slope instability
- **Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice)** is rated **Hazard Level: Medium** with **Hazard Index: 2**, due to **flash flooding and drainage overload affecting track operations**.
- **Landslide** is also **Hazard Level: Medium** with **Hazard Index: 2**, caused by **slope instability triggered by intense precipitation events**.

These hazards suggest that intense rainfall and associated geotechnical effects can disrupt railway operations and damage assets.

## Low hazards

### Wind effects
- **Changing wind patterns** has **Hazard Level: Low** and **Hazard Index: 1**, with only **minor crosswind effects on train stability**.

This indicates limited sensitivity to gradual changes in wind patterns in the provided assessment.

## No variation and not applicable hazards

### No variation
- **Changing precipitation patterns** is classified as **Hazard Level: No variation** with **Hazard Index: 0**, indicating **limited change in total precipitation; minimal direct operational impact**.

### Not applicable
The following drivers are marked **Not applicable** because the table indicates no relevant hazard variation for this infrastructure:
- **Cold waves/frost** — **Hazard Index: -**
- **Wildfires** — **Hazard Index: -**
- **Storm (winds)** — **Hazard Index: -**

## Key interpretation
Overall, the railway system is most exposed to:
1. **Extreme heat**
2. **Drought and water stress**
3. **Moderate flooding and landslide-related disruption**

By contrast, **cold waves/frost, wildfires, and storm (winds)** are not considered relevant hazards in this table, and **changing precipitation patterns** show **No variation**.

# Climate Hazards Affecting Railway Infrastructure
## Thermal Extremes and Fatigue

The changing temperature (air) is a significant climate driver for railway infrastructure, with a Hazard Index of 2 and a Medium Hazard Level. This is due to thermal expansion and structural stress on rail components over time. Temperature variability also poses a moderate risk, with a similar Impact model indicating cumulative thermal fatigue on rail and overhead infrastructure.

## Extreme Weather Events

Heat waves have a substantial impact on railway operations, with a Hazard Index of 5 and an Extreme Hazard Level. Track buckling and equipment failure due to extreme heat are major concerns. In contrast, cold waves/frost, while potentially hazardous, do not pose a relevant hazard variation (Hazard Index: -).

## Water-Related Hazards

Changing precipitation patterns have minimal direct operational impact, with a Hazard Index of 0 and No Variation in the Hazard Level. However, water stress is a significant concern, with a Hazard Index of 4 and a Very High Hazard Level. Reduced water availability for maintenance and cooling operations can compromise railway operations.

## Geological Hazards

Droughts have an Extreme Hazard Level (Hazard Index: 5) due to soil desiccation affecting embankment stability and vegetation. Heavy precipitation events, including flash flooding and drainage overload, pose a moderate risk (Hazard Index: 2). Landslide triggered by intense precipitation events also falls into the Medium category (Hazard Index: 2).

## Low-Risk Hazards

Changing wind patterns have minor crosswind effects on train stability, with a Hazard Index of 1 and a Low Hazard Level. Storms (winds) do not pose a relevant hazard variation (-), while wildfires are also negligible in this context.

The analysis highlights the critical importance of addressing thermal extremes, water-related hazards, and geological risks to ensure the resilience and reliability of railway infrastructure under climate change scenarios.

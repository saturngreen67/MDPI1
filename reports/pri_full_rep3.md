## Potential Risk Index (PRI) Assessment Report

This report details the Potential Risk Index (PRI) assessment for various infrastructure impacts, derived from the Hazard Index (HI), Exposure Index (EI), and Vulnerability Index (VI). The PRI is calculated as HI x EI x VI, normalized to a 0-4 scale. A default EI of 3 is applied uniformly where economic asset valuation is unavailable.

### High and Moderate Risks (PRI 2)

The highest PRI scores of 2 are associated with **Landslides and debris flow** and **Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice)**.

*   **Landslides and debris flow:**
    *   **Drivers:** These risks are characterized by a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2), reflecting a notable frequency or intensity of landslides, coupled with high Exposure (EI: 4) and very high Vulnerability (VI: 5). The high VI is attributed to the severe consequences of landslides, including reactive CAPEX for damages and potential operational stoppages.
    *   **Consequences:** Impacts include increased CAPEX for repairs and maintenance, and potential revenue loss due to operational disruptions.

*   **Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice):**
    *   **Drivers:** This category presents a PRI of 2 due to a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) and high Exposure (EI: 4), combined with a high Vulnerability (VI: 4). The vulnerability stems from the specific impact on the track bed (ballast), necessitating increased maintenance expenditure.
    *   **Consequences:** The primary consequence is an increase in Operational Expenditure (OPEX) due to heightened maintenance requirements.

### Low Risks (PRI 1)

Risks with a PRI of 1 are associated with **Temperature variability**, **Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice)**, and **Changing wind patterns**.

*   **Temperature variability:**
    *   **Drivers:** This risk has a low Hazard Index (HI: 1) and high Exposure (EI: 4), with a moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3).
    *   **Consequences:** The impact is an increase in OPEX due to maintenance needs.

*   **Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice):**
    *   **Drivers:** This scenario exhibits a moderate Hazard Index (HI: 2) and high Exposure (EI: 4), with a moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3).
    *   **Consequences:** The primary concern is the potential for revenue loss due to operational stoppages.

*   **Changing wind patterns:**
    *   **Drivers:** This risk is characterized by a low Hazard Index (HI: 1) and high Exposure (EI: 4), with a low Vulnerability (VI: 2).
    *   **Consequences:** Similar to heavy precipitation, the main impact is potential revenue loss from operational interruptions.

### No Risk (PRI 0)

*   **Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice):**
    *   **Drivers:** This impact shows a Hazard Index of 0 (HI: 0), indicating no significant hazard variation, combined with high Exposure (EI: 4) and moderate Vulnerability (VI: 3).
    *   **Consequences:** The assessed impact is an increase in OPEX due to maintenance, but the absence of a hazard driver results in a PRI of 0.

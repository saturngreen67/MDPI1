# Potential Risk Index (PRI) Assessment Report

This assessment integrates the **Hazard Index (HI)**, **Exposure Index (EI)**, and **Vulnerability Index (VI)** to derive the **Potential Risk Index (PRI)** on a 0–4 scale. In this dataset, **EI is uniformly 4** across all entries, indicating consistently high exposure. The reported PRI values are used as provided in the source table.

## Highest Risks

### PRI 2 — Landslides and related precipitation-driven impacts
The highest risk level in the dataset is **PRI 2**, associated with several impacts driven by **Landslides** and precipitation-related conditions.

#### 1) Reactive CAPEX due to damages associated to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

This is the most severe combination in the table, with a moderate hazard level, high exposure, and very high vulnerability.

#### 2) Increased maintenance due to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

The risk is elevated by the same hazard profile as above, with the same high exposure and very high vulnerability.

#### 3) Increased maintenance for damage to the track bed (ballast) due to changes in precipitation
- **Hazard:** Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice)
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 4
- **PRI:** 2

Here, the hazard remains moderate, exposure is high, and vulnerability is high, resulting in a PRI of 2.

#### 4) Stop of operations due to landslides and debris flow
- **Hazard:** Landslides
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 5
- **PRI:** 2

Operational disruption risk is driven by the same hazard-exposure-vulnerability profile as the maintenance and CAPEX impacts above.

## Moderate to Low Risks

### PRI 1 — Temperature, wind, and heavy precipitation-related operational impacts
These impacts are assessed as **PRI 1**, reflecting either lower hazard intensity or lower vulnerability relative to the highest-risk entries.

#### 1) Increased maintenance due to temperature increase
- **Hazard:** Temperature variability
- **Climate indicator:** Mean annual temperature
- **HI:** 1
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 3
- **PRI:** 1

The hazard level is low, while exposure remains high and vulnerability is moderate, producing a low overall PRI.

#### 2) Stop of operations due to strong winds
- **Hazard:** Changing wind patterns
- **Climate indicator:** Annual mean windspeed at 10 m
- **HI:** 1
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 2
- **PRI:** 1

The lower hazard and relatively lower vulnerability constrain the PRI despite high exposure.

#### 3) Stop of operations due to heavy rain
- **Hazard:** Heavy precipitation (rain, hail, snow/ice)
- **Climate indicator:** Number of days with heavy rain (days/year)
- **HI:** 2
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 3
- **PRI:** 1

Although the hazard is moderate and exposure is high, the vulnerability is lower than in the PRI 2 cases, resulting in a lower overall risk score.

## No Risk

### PRI 0 — Chronic precipitation change
#### Increased maintenance due to increase in precipitation
- **Hazard:** Changing precipitation patterns and types (rain, hail, snow/ice)
- **Climate indicator:** Mean annual rainfall (including frozen precipitation)
- **HI:** 0
- **EI:** 4
- **VI:** 3
- **PRI:** 0

A HI of 0 indicates no relevant hazard variation for this driver. In accordance with the protocol, this is treated as not applicable for risk escalation, and the resulting PRI is 0.

## Conclusion

The dataset shows that the **highest PRI values (PRI 2)** are concentrated in **landslide-related impacts** and in one precipitation-related maintenance impact where both hazard and vulnerability are elevated. **Operational stoppages due to heavy rain, strong winds, and temperature variability** are assessed at **PRI 1**, reflecting lower hazard intensity and/or lower vulnerability. One chronic precipitation-related maintenance driver records **PRI 0** because the hazard index is zero, indicating no relevant hazard variation for that driver.
